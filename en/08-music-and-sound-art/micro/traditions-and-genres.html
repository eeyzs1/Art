MICRO / 08 · Traditions and Genres

The History of Genres Is Not an Evolution

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Traditions and Genres
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

The swing of jazz, the timbre synthesis of electronic music, the yin-nao ornaments (vibrato and portamento) of the guqin (seven-string zither): each tradition has its own core parameters, and one ruler cannot rank them all.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Music and Sound Art; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Jazz: Swing feel and the grammar of harmonic improvisation: swing comes from the uneven division of eighth notes and the steady groove of the rhythm section, not from rushing.
  • Electronic music: Synthesis parameters become the "orchestration" directly: filter and envelope are the playing.
  • Oral transmission in traditional music: The tension between oral transmission and notation: before recordings, the very notion of a "version" was different.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Listen to one minute each of two different traditions; for each, write down "the one most important parameter" and give time-point evidence.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

The two parameters differ and each carries evidence; "both sound lovely" does not count.
