LESSON 08.01

Meter and Rhythm

Lesson 1 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Meter and Rhythm" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

A steady pulse serves as a reference in time; rhythm is the organization of long and short among sounds and rests. Tempo affects the spacing of the pulse; a rhythmic pattern can keep the same relations at different tempos.

Reading anchor: Carnegie Hall: Music Educators Toolbox. Choose Rhythm & Meter or Singing and follow the exercises set by the institution itself. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

Clap four times in a row, then replace the second clap with a rest: the underlying pulse can continue while the actual arrangement of sounds has changed.

Hear the beat

Turn on the sound and count four beats, then clap only on beats 1 and 3; the higher pitch only marks beat one. After stopping, try to keep the beat on your own.

Waiting to start

03 · Do one exercise

Practice with the beat tool on the page: clap on every beat, then on every other beat, counting the beats steadily as you go.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: Tempo changes the intervals of time; it does not change what the instrument is made of.
