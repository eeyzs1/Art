LESSON 04.02

Light and Structure

Lesson 2 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Light and Structure" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Columns, walls, and beams can carry loads and can also organize the rhythm of space. Structural systems follow two basic strategies: with load-bearing walls, openings are restricted; with a column-and-beam frame, the loads pass into the skeleton and the walls are set free. Arches and domes use curves to carry the weight to supports on either side, while trusses and suspension cables span wide distances through the tension and compression of their members. Natural light changes with the hour, and material surfaces alter its reflection and its feel. Here we only practice appreciation; no judgment of structural safety is being made.

Reading anchor: V&A: collection search. Search sculpture, architecture, or furniture and choose objects with materials and dimensions recorded. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

A window cut in the side of a paper box and a hole cut in its top distribute light differently; this helps in understanding how light shapes space.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Cut two openings at different places in a paper box and compare the reach of the light with a flashlight.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: The source of light and its change over time are observable variables.
