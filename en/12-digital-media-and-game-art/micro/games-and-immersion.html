Mechanics Are Rhetoric

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Games and Immersion
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

Rules of play carry values: scarce resources teach trade-offs, the cost of failure teaches caution; "mechanics are rhetoric" is the key to this group of entries.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Digital Media and Game Art; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Level design: Teaching without words: a good first three minutes teaches the basic controls without any manual.
  • Serious games: Goals turned outward: training and educational games cannot be judged by the metrics of entertainment.
  • Virtual reality: Embodiment and motion sickness: the body becomes the interface, and comfort is a design floor, not a concession.
  • Game genres: Clusters of rule conventions — platformer, role-playing, strategy, shooter: genre is shorthand for a design tradition, and innovation often happens where genres cross.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Analyze a game you know well: write down one "value" that a mechanic conveys, then design a change that makes it convey the opposite value.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

The change happens at the level of rules, not at the level of copy.
