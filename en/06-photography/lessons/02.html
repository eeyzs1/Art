LESSON 06.02

Time and Light

Lesson 2 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Time and Light" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Exposure is set by three controls together: the shutter governs how long light enters and how movement is traced; the aperture governs the size of the opening and depth of field; ISO governs sensitivity and noise. Changing one can be offset with the other two, but each control leaves its side effects in the picture. The direction of light shapes shadow and texture, and no single parameter works as a universal formula for style.

02 · Study one example

Side light often makes surface relief more visible, while frontal light may weaken the differences of shadow; the exact effect needs to be compared on the same object.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Photograph one cup in window light, turning the cup or moving the camera, and compare the shadows.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: Hold the other conditions steady, so the effect of light can be isolated.
