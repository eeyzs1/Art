LESSON 00.01

Turn Feeling Into Evidence

Lesson 1 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Turn Feeling Into Evidence" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Description points to positions, sounds, or movements that anyone can locate; interpretation is an inference about meaning; evaluation is a judgment made against standards. The three can support one another, but they must not be conflated. Saying "the picture feels lonely" is not enough: show how the spacing between the figures, the direction of the gazes, or the empty areas support that feeling.

Reading anchor: Getty: formal analysis. See the definitions of line, shape, space, and color with examples from the collection. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

A chair placed near the edge of the frame with a wide expanse of empty space around it: that is an observation, while "it suggests exclusion" is an interpretation. The emptiness might instead highlight the scale of the space, so the interpretation must leave other possibilities open.

Observation lab: change one variable

After dragging, first describe the relationship of the shapes, then propose an interpretation. This is a simplified demonstration, not a substitute for real materials or artworks.

03 · Do one exercise

Observe one object near you and write three descriptions, two interpretations, and one question to verify later.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: Distinguish the visible evidence of the work from the artist's psychology; the latter requires documents.

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
