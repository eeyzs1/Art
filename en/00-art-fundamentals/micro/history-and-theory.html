Theory Is a Toolbox for Asking Questions

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · History and Theory
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

The entries in this group all answer one question: how do we ask? Art history asks when, where, and how a work was used; aesthetics asks where artistic value comes from; criticism asks whether the work holds up under specific criteria. Theory is not jargon to be applied; it is a way of choosing evidence.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Art Fundamentals; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Historiography of art history: The discipline's own history: why did "the Renaissance" enter the general textbooks before "Ming painting"? Whoever wrote the first textbook set the default point of view.
  • Iconography: From "I see a Madonna" to "why this Madonna": interpretation must rest on accurate description checked against cultural conventions.
  • Comparative art studies: Comparison needs a shared question, not a ranking: first ask "what problem is each of them solving," then speak of similarities and differences.
  • The four theories of art: The theory of imitation (mimesis) asks whether art re-presents the world; expression theory asks whether it expresses emotion; formalism asks whether form itself carries meaning; the institutional theory of art asks how the art world recognizes an object — the same work will be asked four different questions.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Choose one work and write one question in each of three modes — art history, aesthetics, criticism: when, where, and by whom was it used / on what grounds does it hold together / how does it measure up under my criteria.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

The three questions each do their own job; none substitutes for or blurs into another.
