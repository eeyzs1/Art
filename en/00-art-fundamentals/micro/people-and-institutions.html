MICRO / 00 · People and Institutions

Who Pays, Who Watches, Who Defines

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · People and Institutions
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

The shared question of this group: art is not made in a vacuum. Commissioning, education, dissemination, management, and policy together decide what gets made, what gets seen, and what gets remembered.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Art Fundamentals; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Sociology of art: Taste has social coordinates: who has leisure, who has education, and who enters the gallery all shape the default answer to "what is good art."
  • Art market research: Price and value are often out of alignment: scarcity, auction history, and institutional authentication constitute a logic of their own.
  • Cultural policy: An intangible-heritage list both protects and reshapes: once an item is listed, the form of its transmission also changes.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Look up a work you view often and write down four questions: who made it, who funded it, who had the right to view it at the time, and who can see it now.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

All four questions have sources; for anything you cannot find, write "unknown" plainly.
