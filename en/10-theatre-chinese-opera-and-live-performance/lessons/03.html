LESSON 10.03

The Stage Is a Collaborative Art

Lesson 3 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "The Stage Is a Collaborative Art" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Director, actors, stage design, lighting, costume, and sound organize the watching together. The words of the script and a particular performance are not the same object; comparing performances should record the production version.

Reading anchor: V&A: Theatre and Performance collection. Watch the collaborative process through costume, stage design, and performance archives; this British collection is no substitute for specialized material on Chinese xiqu. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

A dimming of the lights and a long pause before one line can shift the audience's expectations, but the meaning still depends on what comes before and after.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Write two versions of lighting and blocking notes for a two-line exchange and explain the relationship you mean to change.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: The production version helps distinguish different stage treatments.

04 · Quick self-check

What must be recorded when comparing performances?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
