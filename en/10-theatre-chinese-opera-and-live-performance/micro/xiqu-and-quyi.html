MICRO / 10 · Xiqu and Quyi

Conventions Are Compressed Language

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Xiqu and Quyi
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

Qiba (armoring the general), tangma (riding the horse), water sleeves: conventions compress complex experience into units that can be learned and recombined; understanding conventions is like understanding grammar, not memorizing words.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Role categories (hangdang): A typed system of acting, voice, and stage appearance: the role category is an entrance, not a cage.
  • Narrative in quyi: The craft, in quyi (narrative telling and singing), of one teller carrying many roles: the comedic "bundles" (baofu) of pingshu storytelling are precision designs in timing.
  • The shuimo ("water-polished") style: A strict correspondence of syllable sound and melody: setting the tune by the word sounds keeps the sung text always clear.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Watch a passage of Peking opera or Kunqu opera; identify one conventionalized action, and write down what experience it compresses and what fundamental training it demands.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

The identification reaches a concrete action and role category, not loose talk of "national treasure."
