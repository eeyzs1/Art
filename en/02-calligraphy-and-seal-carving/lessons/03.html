LESSON 02.03

Positive and Negative on the Seal Face

Lesson 3 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Positive and Negative on the Seal Face" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Seal carving involves script, knife work, material, and the layout of the seal face. Zhuwen (relief) usually presents red characters; baiwen (intaglio) usually presents white characters; the organization of red and white, together with the border and any breaks, shapes what we see.

Reading anchor: The Palace Museum: Zhao Mengfu, Luoshen fu in running script. Look together with the museum's notes on character structure, strokes, and layout. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

Within one square, keeping the red lines or cutting them away reverses the relation between characters and ground. An on-screen simulation is no substitute for a real stamped impression.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Draw two seal faces of your initials on paper, one in red characters and one in white, using no knife.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: In baiwen the characters usually appear in white against the red seal face.

04 · Quick self-check

What does a baiwen (intaglio) seal usually present?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
