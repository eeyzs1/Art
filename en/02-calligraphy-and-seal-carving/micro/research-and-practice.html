MICRO / 02 · Research and Practice

Copying Is Analysis, Not Photocopying

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Research and Practice
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

These entries form a methodology of study: a hierarchy of observation running from brushwork to overall composition, the relation between copying and assembling characters, and the two great traditions of study, steles and modelbooks.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Calligraphy and Seal Carving; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Analytical copying: Extract only one problem per pass: this pass watches only the turns, the next only the spacing.
  • Assembling characters (jizi): An exercise in organizing a new composition without the context of the original: it trains judgment of relations, not single characters.
  • Stele studies and modelbook studies: The mediating difference between carved marks and ink traces: a stele's square crispness may come from the knife and from weathering, not from the brush.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Choose a model of five characters; make a spatial analysis diagram first, then copy it once, and write two notes on the deviations.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

Deviations are described down to relations between concrete components — not as "lacking spirit resonance."
