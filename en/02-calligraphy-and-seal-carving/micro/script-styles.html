MICRO / 02 · Script Styles

Five Coexisting Scripts, Not a Relay

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Script Styles
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

Script evolution is often told as a straight line "from seal script to cursive, ever more convenient"; in fact the styles coexisted in functional layers: solemn occasions used formal scripts, daily writing used cursive, and every style stayed alive in every period.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Calligraphy and Seal Carving; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Formal and cursive scripts: Stele carvings use formal scripts, letters use running and cursive: the occasion decides the script, and the script in turn marks the occasion.
  • The clerical transformation (libian): The structural turn of Chinese characters from seal script toward clerical script: conversion into strokes and simplification is an event in the history of writing, not an aesthetic movement.
  • Cursive script: The barrier to reading is real: it is a conventional system for high-speed writing, not arbitrary marks.
  • Recognizing the five script styles: Seal script is even-textured and elongated; clerical script carries the wavelike flaring stroke ("silkworm head, swallow tail"); regular script is squared; running script links strokes; cursive script turns them into signs: recognize the forms first, discuss style afterward.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Find one work in seal script and one in regular (or running) script; record in each one structural feature that exists "for speed" or "for solemnity."

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

Features are stated in terms of strokes and structure, and the two works are compared.
