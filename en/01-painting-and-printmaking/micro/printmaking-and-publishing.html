MICRO / 01 · Printmaking and Publishing

The Matrix Is the Mother of Multiples

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Printmaking and Publishing
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

A print's image comes from the contact of the matrix with paper. Plate wear, edition numbers, and signing conventions all remind you: what you see is one impression, not a unique original.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Painting and Printmaking; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • The four print processes: Relief (woodcut) and intaglio (engraving and etching) run on opposite black-and-white logic: one cuts away the white and keeps the black, the other bites recesses that hold the ink; lithography images through the mutual repulsion of oil and water; screenprint pushes ink through the open mesh by squeegee pressure — the process decides the character of the impression and of the multiple.
  • Edition number and plate wear: Number 3/50 and number 49/50 may differ visibly in state: the edition number is both a system of honesty and a mechanism of value.
  • Artists' books: The book as a work, not a container: page order, layout, and binding are all creative decisions.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Find a print in any collection and record its process and edition information; if either is missing, write "unknown" and list the places you checked.
