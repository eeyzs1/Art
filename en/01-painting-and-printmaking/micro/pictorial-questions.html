MICRO / 01 · Pictorial Questions

Subject Matter Is a Question, Not a List

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Pictorial Questions
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

A portrait is never only "a good likeness": it handles identity, commemoration, and power. Shanshui (landscape) painting and Western landscape painting are two programs for viewing nature; still life turns "things" into the theme.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Painting and Printmaking; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Shanshui and landscape: Wandering viewing versus the fixed point: the moving viewpoint of the handscroll and fixed-viewpoint perspective are two spatial rhetorics.
  • Portraiture: The social function beyond likeness: who was portrayed, what they wear, and where they look are all statements of identity.
  • History painting: The choice of narrative climax: which instant is painted decides which layer of the story the viewer sees.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Choose one portrait and one shanshui (or landscape) and answer for each: "what does it want the viewer to do?"

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

Evidence comes from composition, format, and orientation; do not answer from the artist's psychology.
