LESSON 01.03

Materials Speak Too

Lesson 3 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Materials Speak Too" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

The covering power of pigment, the absorbency of paper, and the transfer of printing each leave different traces. A print usually produces its image through the relation between block and paper and cannot simply be read as a reproduction of a painting.

Reading anchor: Getty: formal analysis. See the definitions of line, shape, space, and color with examples from the collection. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

Transparent layering in watercolor and the covering power of thickly applied paint meet different limits when changes are made; a limit can also become a resource for style.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Draw one tree each with the side of a pencil, with the point, and by rubbing onto paper, and compare the traces.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: Material analysis attends to process and effect; price is not sufficient evidence of artistic quality.

04 · Quick self-check

What should a discussion of materials focus on?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
