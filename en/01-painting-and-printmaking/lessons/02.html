LESSON 01.02

Color and Space

Lesson 2 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Color and Space" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Hue distinguishes families of color, value distinguishes light from dark, and saturation distinguishes vividness; when the hues are arranged in a circle, the two colors opposite each other are complementary and contrast most strongly side by side. Space can be rendered by overlapping, size, perspective, or layering; one-point perspective must not be treated as the only correct way of depicting space.

Reading anchor: Getty: formal analysis. See the definitions of line, shape, space, and color with examples from the collection. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

An ink shanshui (landscape) painting can organize space through overlapping mountains and a path for the eye, an experience different from a perspective drawing at a fixed viewpoint.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

With a single brush, paint three ranges of mountains, changing only the overlaps and the density, without any help from color.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: A foreground covering a background is a spatial clue; it does not directly document the artist's life.

04 · Quick self-check

What clue does overlapping mainly supply?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
