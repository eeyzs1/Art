MICRO / 09 · Practices and Traditions

Genealogies of Bodily Technique

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Practices and Traditions
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

The open, stretched, upright line of ballet, the shenyun (body rhythm) of Chinese classical dance, the groove of street dance — each trains a different set of bodily defaults; when watching, first identify "what this body is working to solve."

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Dance and Body-Based Art; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Ballet: Systematizing the vertical axis and turnout: the center of weight is trained onto a precise, narrow band.
  • Street dance: Groove comes before a list of steps: a continuous response to the music comes first, steps after.
  • Contact improvisation: An improvised grammar of weight and shared points of balance: trust is a technique, not an attitude.
  • Folk and ethnic dance: Take the courtyard figures and gong-and-drum cues of yangge (folk dance): gait, formation, and instruments together compose a local body memory; when watching, first identify "what this body is working to solve."

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Watch one minute each of two dance traditions; in each, record three "bodily defaults": center of weight, way of initiating force, relation to the music.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

The description stays with facts of the body, without remarks like "more elegant."
