LESSON 09.01

Body, Space, and Time

Lesson 1 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Body, Space, and Time" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Dance can be observed through body parts, movement, space, time, and effort. Record what happened first and discuss emotion afterwards; no single movement carries one fixed meaning across all cultures.

Reading anchor: BC curriculum: elements of dance. Check the observation dimensions of body, space, time, and effort. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

An extended hand can invite, block, or search; direction, speed, and the distance from others change how it reads.

Observation lab: change one variable

After dragging, first describe the relationship of the shapes, then propose an interpretation. This is a simplified demonstration, not a substitute for real materials or artworks.

03 · Do one exercise

Seated, extend and withdraw your hand, once slowly and once suddenly, and record the difference in what you feel.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: Observe the movement first; propose emotional interpretations afterwards.

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
