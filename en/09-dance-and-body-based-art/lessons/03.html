LESSON 09.03

Culture and Watching

Lesson 3 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Culture and Watching" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Dance may take part in ritual, in sociability, on the stage, or in the expression of identity. When watching different traditions, consult the accounts of performers and communities and avoid treating an unfamiliar movement as a token of the exotic.

Reading anchor: BC curriculum: elements of dance. Check the observation dimensions of body, space, time, and effort. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

The same step in a community celebration and on a theater stage may differ in how people join in and how closely they watch.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Watch a dance published by an established institution and record the occasion, the participants, and three pieces of movement evidence.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: Context helps explain what the movement means in its own setting.

04 · Quick self-check

How should cross-cultural watching proceed?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
