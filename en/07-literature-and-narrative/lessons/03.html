LESSON 07.03

Close Reading and Comparison

Lesson 3 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Close Reading and Comparison" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Close reading starts from words, syntax, and structure; comparison defines a shared question. A translation carries the translator's choices, so cross-language comparison should state the edition and avoid taking a translated line as the voice of the original.

Reading anchor: Aristotle, Poetics (English translation). Read selected passages on poetry and action; this is one historical and theoretical perspective and does not stand for all literary traditions. Close reading in this unit still rests on the primary works in the edition you hold, with the version stated. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

Comparing two poems about rain, ask whether the rain is background, an obstacle to action, or a trigger of feeling, rather than only which poem is more beautiful.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Choose a classical poem you know well, circle its verbs, and explain what would be lost if one of them were replaced.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: A shared question gives the comparison a basis that can be discussed.

04 · Quick self-check

What must be settled before comparing works?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
