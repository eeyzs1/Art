MICRO / 13 · Art in Everyday Life

Use Comes Before Display

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Art in Everyday Life
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

Paper-cut window flowers are pasted on windows, masks are worn on faces: the first site of these forms is use, not the showcase; materials and craft obey the needs of the occasion.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Folk Art and Intermedia; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • New Year prints (nianhua): Solar terms and door gods: the calendar of the year shapes the subjects, and the place of pasting shapes the composition; one block prints one color, and the count of blocks is a clue to the craft.
  • Lanterns: An engineering of light, structure, and moving by night: translucency and the bearing of weight are real physical problems.
  • Folk narrative: The fluidity of the oral version: there is no single "definitive text," and the variant tellings are themselves historical evidence.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Document one festive object in your home: who uses it, when, and where it goes afterward; add an account of its provenance and note whether it is firsthand or hearsay.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

You distinguish the functional chain of the object from the evidential grade of its provenance story.
