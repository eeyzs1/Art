LESSON 11.01

The Shot Selects Information

Lesson 1 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "The Shot Selects Information" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Shot scale controls the proportion of figure to surroundings; camera movements such as zoom, dolly, pan, tracking, and following, together with the angle, change the viewing position and the pace. The shot is a unit that selects information, and its work cannot be summed up as "looking good."

Reading anchor: BFI: reading a short film or scene. Use the BFI question sheet to watch shots, editing, and sound. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

A long shot lets us see the figure in relation to its surroundings; a close shot can emphasize a face or an object; neither carries a fixed emotional effect.

Shot-order experiment

① Face: a person looks offscreen
② Object: an untouched meal on the table
③ Space: an empty chair
Which parts do you see, and which do you infer?

03 · Do one exercise

Film the same cup in a long shot and a close shot and state what information each one supplies.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: Shot scale changes the range of information; meaning still depends on context.

04 · Quick self-check

What does a change of shot scale directly affect?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
