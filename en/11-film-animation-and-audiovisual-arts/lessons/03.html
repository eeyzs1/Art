LESSON 11.03

Sound and Animated Time

Lesson 3 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Sound and Animated Time" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Onscreen (diegetic) sound has a visible source in the frame; the source of offscreen sound is not shown; sounds inside and outside the story world must also be told apart. Animation produces the experience of movement through the timing of successive images.

Reading anchor: BFI: reading a short film or scene. Use the BFI question sheet to watch shots, editing, and sound. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

An empty corridor with footsteps makes the viewer expect someone to approach; sound can supply information the picture never shows.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Design two sound treatments for a ten-second static scene and state what each leads the viewer to expect.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: Whether a source is visible and whether it belongs to the story world are different questions.

04 · Quick self-check

What else does sound analysis need to consider?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
