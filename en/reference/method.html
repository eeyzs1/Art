How to Look: A Method Card

Five steps, usable anywhere

  1. Data: title, creator or community, date, medium, size or duration, function, source. Write "unknown" wherever something is unknown.
  2. Observation: what exactly do I see, hear, or experience? List three pieces of evidence first.
  3. Relations: how do repetition, contrast, emphasis, rhythm, narrative order, or rules of participation organize the experience?
  4. Context: who made, funded, used, and viewed it? What do I still need to verify?
  5. Judgment: what are my standards? What other reasonable interpretation is there?

Four self-check items, 0–2 points each

The total only helps you find the next step; it is not a ranking of taste. Below 6 points, strengthen the weakest item first.

Six everyday terms

Medium: the material, technique, or form on which expression rests. Composition: the organization of elements within the picture. Rhythm: repetition and change of events in time or space. Context: the concrete background of making, use, circulation, and viewing. Form: the perceptible organization of the work. Criticism: discussing a work with evidence and standards, not merely finding fault.
