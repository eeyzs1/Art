00 · Art Fundamentals

Aesthetics

The branch of philosophy that studies aesthetic experience and the value of art; answers to "what counts as art" differ from culture to culture, and there is more than one aesthetic tradition.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Historiography of art history

The study of how the discipline of art history itself took shape: which questions, methods, and biases formed it (for example, Vasari's biographical tradition of artists and Winckelmann's stylistic periodization).

Category: Art Fundamentals

Iconography

Asks after the layers of meaning an image motif carries in a particular culture; Panofsky distinguished three steps: pre-iconographic description, iconographic analysis, and iconological interpretation.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Formal analysis

A method that organizes description solely from visible form (line, color, composition, traces of material); powerful, but it does not explain origin or function.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Style

A set of recognizable features shared by a group of works; it may attach to an individual, period, region, or school, and its boundaries are a research tool, not a natural category.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Patronage

Who pays, and for whom a work is made, shapes art profoundly: court, church, guild, gallery, and market each imply different conditions of creation.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Art market

The mechanisms of dealing, auction, galleries, and pricing; prices reflect scarcity and institutional recognition, not a direct measure of artistic value.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Preventive conservation

Extending the life of collections by controlling temperature and humidity, light, and handling; "restoration," by contrast, raises ethical debate over the degree of intervention.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Theory of imitation (mimesis)

One of the oldest theories of art: art imitates or represents the world; concepts of "imitation" differ across traditions - Aristotle's mimesis and Xie He's "transmission by copying" are not the same thing.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Expression theory

Holds that the value of art lies in expressing emotion or inner experience; contrasted with imitation since nineteenth-century Romanticism, while the Chinese "poetry articulates intent" (shi yan zhi) and "follows from feeling" (yuan qing) form a separate genealogy of expression.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Formalism

A theoretical orientation holding that the meaning of art comes chiefly from its formal organization (Bell's "significant form," Russian Formalism); it supported formal analysis and has been criticized for ignoring context.

Category: Art Fundamentals

Institutional theory of art

A position in twentieth-century analytic aesthetics: whether something is art depends on its designation by the artworld; Duchamp's readymades are its most frequently cited case.

Category: Art Fundamentals

01 · Painting and Printmaking

Cunfa (texture strokes)

The brush-method system by which landscape painting renders the texture and structure of rock and mountain (hemp-fiber strokes, axe-cut strokes, and others); different cunfa correspond to different geologies and traditions of seeing.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

The three distances (sanyuan)

Proposed in Guo Xi's Linquan gaozhi (The Lofty Message of Forests and Streams): high, deep, and level distance describe how shanshui painting organizes viewpoints and wandering viewing, unlike fixed-viewpoint perspective.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Blue-and-green landscape

The landscape tradition built on azurite blue and malachite green as its principal colors, at its height in Song dynasty academy painting; together with ink monochrome it forms the two great systems of color.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Literati painting

The painting tradition of the scholar class, stressing that calligraphy and painting share one source, poetic meaning, and the expression of character; authorship and the colophon system are part of the tradition itself.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Colophons and seals

Inscriptions added by later hands and collectors' seals on a painting constitute its history of transmission and are themselves important historical evidence.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Linear perspective

The system of spatial construction developed in the Renaissance on the assumption of a single fixed viewpoint; it is one effective spatial system among others, not the inevitable endpoint of "progress."

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Egg tempera

Painting with pigment tempered in egg, which dries quickly in transparent layers and was common in the early Renaissance; the arrival of oil painting extended the possibilities of revision and blending.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Multi-block color printing

Printing in registered colors from separate blocks; the Ming Ten Bamboo Studio Manual of Calligraphy and Painting and the multi-color printing of Japanese ukiyo-e are its high points.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Impressionism

The group of painters in 1870s-80s Paris concerned with outdoor light, instantaneous vision, and the juxtaposition of brushstrokes; the name came from the mockery aimed at Monet's Impression, Sunrise.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Modernism

A collective term for the movements from the late nineteenth to the mid-twentieth century that pursued the self-renewal of artistic language (Cubism, abstraction, Constructivism); "modern" does not equal "progress," and each country's path to modernism differed.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

Complementary colors and the color wheel

Hues arranged on a circle: those opposite each other are complementary - strongest in contrast when juxtaposed, mutually weakened when mixed. The color wheel is an observational tool, not a physical law of pigment mixing.

Category: Painting and Printmaking

02 · Calligraphy and Seal Carving

The five script styles

Seal, clerical, regular, running, and cursive script are the conventional classification; their development was not a single line of replacement, and the styles long coexisted.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

The Shenlong copy

One of the Tang tracing copies of the Lanting xu (Orchid Pavilion Preface) that survive (traditionally attributed to a tracing by Feng Chengsu), in the Palace Museum; that the original does not survive is where version-consciousness begins.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

Stele and modelbook studies (beixue and tiexue)

Two paths of study since the Qing dynasty: stele studies prize the metal-and-stone air of carved stone, modelbook studies the fluent lineage transmitted in ink; the rivalry between them shaped later accounts of calligraphy history.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

Centered tip and inclined tip

Where the brush tip travels produces different line qualities; judgments of "upright" and "slanted" in the tradition of calligraphy writing changed with taste from era to era.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

Character structure and overall composition

The organization inside a single character (structure) and the relations across characters, columns, and blank space in a whole work (composition) are two distinct levels of observation.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

Zhuwen and baiwen

A seal cut so the characters print red is zhuwen (relief, red characters), one cut so they print white is baiwen (intaglio, white characters); the red-white distribution together with the border determines the composition of the seal face.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

Carved stone and ink rubbing

Stone inscription passes through repeated mediations - writing, carving, weathering, and inking; the date and workmanship of a rubbing directly shape what can be seen.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

Seal studies

The scholarship of seal-carving schools, seal catalogues, and seal theory; Ming and Qing literati seal carving moved the carved seal from practical use to an independent art.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

Raw and sized xuan paper

Raw xuan absorbs ink and bleeds quickly; sized xuan, treated with alum, does not; the character of the paper directly determines the wet or dry look of a line, so fix the paper before comparing brushwork.

Category: Calligraphy and Seal Carving

03 · Sculpture and Installation

Sculpture in the round and relief

Fully three-dimensional carving viewable from many sides and layered carving attached to its ground; actual works often combine the two.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

Negative space

The intervals between solids; the piercing and the sense of enclosure in a sculpture are partly constituted by negative space.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

Lost-wax casting

A casting process in which the wax model is melted out and replaced by metal, making complex curved surfaces possible; the common method of bronze casting.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

Readymade

The practice Duchamp proposed in the 1910s: selecting an everyday object and shifting its context constitutes the work; what it questions is the institution of art, not the beauty of the object.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

Kinetic sculpture

Sculpture that introduces movement (mechanical, wind-driven, or moved by the viewer); Calder's wind-driven works are often cited as the example.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

Site-specific art

Works made for a particular place that lose their effect when moved; contrasted with movable works made for galleries.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

Land art

The 1960s-70s practice that takes land, excavation, and remote sites as its medium; often difficult to exhibit, it exists largely as documentation.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

Public art

Art located in public space and often publicly funded; its "publicness" involves commissioning procedures and the right of users to dispute it.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

Mono-ha

A Japanese tendency of the late 1960s centered on the juxtaposed relation of raw materials such as stone and wood, refusing transformation by working them; Sekine Nobuo and Lee Ufan are the commonly cited representatives.

Category: Sculpture and Installation

04 · Architecture and Landscape

Dougong (bracket sets)

The timber component built of stacked blocks and arms that projects the eaves and carries the load down; its evolution is important evidence for periodizing Chinese architecture.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Sunmao (mortise-and-tenon joinery)

Wooden parts joined by interlocking projection and socket, essentially without nails or glue; different joints answer different loads and different needs of assembly and disassembly.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Yingzao fashi (Treatise on Architectural Methods)

The building standard promulgated by Li Jie in the second Chongning year of the Northern Song (1103), which unified design through the cai-fen modular system; the core document for the study of Song architecture.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Jiejing (borrowed scenery)

A garden device that draws the scenery beyond the garden into the view within; Ji Cheng's Yuanye (The Craft of Gardens) systematized this body of garden-making experience.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Courtyard compound and courtyard

Organizing space through courtyards is the basic way Chinese building groups are composed, with hierarchy and orientation expressed in the sequence of courts.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Tailiang and chuandou framing

The two great timber-frame systems of China: beam-lifted (tailiang) frames are common in the north and through-jointed (chuandou) frames in the south, and mixed use is also widespread.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Load-bearing wall and frame

In load-bearing-wall construction the openings are limited; the column-and-beam frame hands the load to the skeleton and the wall recedes to enclosure - this pair of distinctions determines the freedom of plan and elevation.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Arch and dome

Structural systems that carry weight to the side supports through curves: the arch spans an opening, the dome covers a circular space; the Gothic flying buttress is the celebrated answer to its lateral thrust.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Accessibility

Spatial design that lets bodies of different kinds enter and use a place; ramps, signage, and the sound-and-light environment are all parts of it.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

Adaptive reuse

The conservation strategy of giving an old building a new use; "what to keep and what to change" is a continuing argument.

Category: Architecture and Landscape

05 · Craft and Design

Earthenware and porcelain

They differ in clay body, firing temperature, and water absorption; the collective word "ceramics" often hides this basic boundary.

Category: Craft and Design

Blue-and-white porcelain (qinghua)

Porcelain painted in cobalt under the glaze and fired once at high temperature; Yuan and Ming blue-and-white and the export trade shaped a history of global circulation.

Category: Craft and Design

Kiln centers (yaokou)

The system by which the traditional porcelain industry named its kilns after their localities (Yue, Xing, Jingdezhen, and others); body and glaze traits, firing methods, and fuel are the clues for telling them apart.

Category: Craft and Design

Underglaze and overglaze decoration

Painting under the glaze (abrasion-resistant, stable in color) and over the glaze (brilliant, easily worn) are two different craft paths.

Category: Craft and Design

Ming-style furniture

The furniture tradition of the Ming through the early Qing, distinguished by hardwood mortise-and-tenon joinery; moldings and proportion are its design language.

Category: Craft and Design

Warp and weft, and figured weaving

Cloth comes into being as warp and weft interlace; figured weaving controls the warp threads to weave pattern - the structure of the loom determines which patterns are possible.

Category: Craft and Design

The Bauhaus

The German school of design and art, 1919-1933, that sought the unity of art and technology; the Dessau building and the foundation course had far-reaching influence.

Category: Craft and Design

The grid system

The graphic-design method of organizing a page on a grid; a cornerstone of twentieth-century International Typographic Style.

Category: Craft and Design

Ergonomics

The study of designing objects and environments to the scale and behavior of the human body; together with the definition of the task it forms the basis of design decisions.

Category: Craft and Design

06 · Photography

Aperture and depth of field

The aperture controls the light admitted and the depth of field; the wider the aperture, the shallower the depth, and the stronger the sense of the subject separating from the background.

Category: Photography

Shutter and sensitivity

The shutter controls how long light enters - a long exposure cannot freeze motion - and sensitivity (ISO) marks responsiveness to light, with noise growing more visible as it rises; with aperture these form the three exposure parameters.

Category: Photography

Focal length and angle of view

A short focal length gives a wide angle and exaggerates depth at close range; a long focal length narrows the view and compresses front-to-back distance. Focal length is the basic parameter of framing and of perspectival relations.

Category: Photography

The decisive moment

Cartier-Bresson's phrase for the brief instant when form and content reach balance; it is one orientation, not a universal law.

Category: Photography

The Zone System

Ansel Adams's method of systematically controlling exposure and development, dividing luminance into ten zones to predict the tones of the image.

Category: Photography

Straight photography

The early-twentieth-century stance against Pictorialist soft focus, advocating the sharp, unarranged "camera eye" (Stieglitz, Strand, and others).

Category: Photography

Cyanotype

A cameraless imaging process using iron salts; Anna Atkins's nineteenth-century botanical plates are an early example.

Category: Photography

Photo agencies

Magnum, founded in 1947: the cooperative model in which photographers hold their own copyright changed how documentary photography is produced.

Category: Photography

The photobook

Photographic expression whose final form is the book; sequence and the rhythm of the turning page are its core design problems.

Category: Photography

07 · Literature and Narrative

Fu, bi, xing

The traditional formulation from the Book of Songs: direct statement (fu), comparison (bi), and evocative beginning (xing) form the basic rhetorical framework of poetry in the Chinese language.

Category: Literature and Narrative

Allusion

Citing the stories or lines of earlier writers to layer meaning; understanding allusion depends on a documentary tradition that can be consulted.

Category: Literature and Narrative

Tonal patterns and rhyme

Regulated verse in Chinese organizes sound through level and oblique tones and rhyming syllables; ci and qu song forms have their own, more complex rules of tune patterns.

Category: Literature and Narrative

The sonnet

A fixed poetic form that originated in Italy and was developed by Petrarch and Shakespeare, known for its rhyme scheme and turn.

Category: Literature and Narrative

Free verse

Poetry released from fixed meter and rhyme; freedom does not mean the absence of rhythm - rhythm passes to syntax and image.

Category: Literature and Narrative

Stream of consciousness

Narrative organized by a character's inner associations rather than external cause and effect; Woolf and Joyce are the frequently cited practitioners.

Category: Literature and Narrative

Intertextuality

Texts always answer other texts; recognizing intertext moves comparison from "resemblance" to evidenced connection.

Category: Literature and Narrative

Story and plot

Events ordered as they occurred form the story; events ordered as told form the plot; flashback and digression change the latter, never the former.

Category: Literature and Narrative

Flat and round characters

Forster's distinction: flat characters are built from a single trait, round characters change and surprise convincingly; each has its own function.

Category: Literature and Narrative

08 · Music and Sound Art

Gongche notation

A notation system written in Chinese characters (shang, chi, gong, fan, liu, wu, yi, and others) that records pitch and beat; different genres use it differently.

Category: Music and Sound Art

The twelve pitch standards and the five notes

The pitch system of traditional Chinese music theory and the five notes gong, shang, jue, zhi, and yu; rotating the tonic and changing mode is the theoretical core.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Mode

Different organizations of the degrees within a scale produce different colors; major and minor are only one modal system among many.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Counterpoint

The rules for combining independent melodic lines; Bach's fugues are the commonly cited paradigm.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Sonata form

The structural principle of exposition, development, and recapitulation, a major way of organizing instrumental music since the late eighteenth century; the name is only an analytic label.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Orchestration

Choosing and combining instruments for a work, where timbre becomes a structural force; Berlioz and Ravel are known for it.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Field recording

The basic method of ethnomusicology: recording, interviewing, and participant observation in the context where the music happens.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Chord and tonality

Several pitches sounding together form a chord; tonality is the framework of hearing that organizes tension and resolution around a central tone, with major and minor its common colors - not all music has a tonal center.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Staff notation and numbered notation

Staff notation marks pitch by lines and spaces, numbered notation (jianpu) marks scale degrees with digits; each has its strengths, and many traditions use neither.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Periodization of music history

Baroque, Classical, Romantic, and the rest are ordering tools of later scholars; their boundaries are blurred and overlapping, and they do not apply to traditions outside Western art music.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Raga and tala

The melodic-mode and rhythmic-cycle systems of Indian classical music; improvisation unfolds within the modal frame - an organizational logic different from functional harmony.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Gamelan

The gong-chime ensemble music of Java and Bali and its instrument system; cyclic structure and tuning types (slendro, pelog) are its core.

Category: Music and Sound Art

Maqam

The melodic-mode system of Middle Eastern music, which prescribes scale, ornament, and path of movement; improvised performance takes it as its grammar.

Category: Music and Sound Art

09 · Dance and Body-Based Art

Laban Movement Analysis

Laban's system for describing movement through body, space, effort, and shape; a shared language for recording and analyzing dance.

Category: Dance and Body-Based Art

Dance notation

Symbol systems that record movement (Labanotation, Benesh notation, and others), allowing choreography to be preserved and reconstructed.

Category: Dance and Body-Based Art

Shenyun (body method and rhythm)

The term for "body method and rhythm" in the Chinese classical dance training system; lift, sink, thrust, and lean are among its basic elements.

Category: Dance and Body-Based Art

The ballet positions of the feet

The five basic positions are the foundation of ballet's technical vocabulary, regularized out of court dance.

Category: Dance and Body-Based Art

Improvisation and the score

Improvisation is not the absence of structure; a "score" states the rules that make improvisation repeatable and open to discussion.

Category: Dance and Body-Based Art

Dance theatre (Tanztheater)

The practice exemplified by Pina Bausch, which brings dance, theatre, scenography, and everyday movement onto the stage.

Category: Dance and Body-Based Art

Dance film and video

Choreographing for the camera or recording dance through it, where camera placement and editing become part of the choreography.

Category: Dance and Body-Based Art

10 · Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

Role categories (hangdang)

The character types of xiqu (sheng, dan, jing, chou, and their subdivisions), each combining performance conventions, voice, and makeup; every genre has its own system.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

The four skills and five methods

Song, speech, acting, and combat (the four skills) and hands, eyes, body, method, and steps (the five methods) are the basic framework of xiqu performance training.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

Shuimo qiang (water-polished melody)

The common name for the singing style of Kunqu, in which the relation between word-sound and melody is strict, the melody shaped by the tones of each word.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

Theatrical convention

The shared fictional agreement between stage and audience; the conventions of xiqu and the Western "fourth wall" are two different schemes of convention.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

The alienation effect

Brecht's demand that spectators keep a critical distance rather than identify completely; it stands against the immersion-oriented Stanislavsky system.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

The three unities

The neoclassical rule that place, time, and action be kept unified; it is a textbook rule, not the essence of tragedy.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

Stage directions

The text in a play script that specifies action, light, and sound; they are evidence of design intent, not the final performance.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

Aria and recitative

In opera, the lyrically complete number and the speech-like passage that carries the plot forward; their alternation forms the breathing of operatic narrative.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

Noh

Japanese masked dance-drama that joins chant, instruments, dance, and song; the jo-ha-kyu structure and an aesthetics of extreme slowing are unique to it.

Category: Theatre, Chinese Opera and Live Performance

11 · Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

The axis of action

The imaginary line set up by the gazes and movements of figures in a scene; cutting across it confuses screen direction - and can also be done deliberately.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

Shot/reverse shot

The editing convention of alternating between the two sides of a dialogue; it shapes where the viewer is placed.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

Montage

The theory and practice of producing meaning by juxtaposing shots; Eisenstein's montage of collision is the classic statement.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

Mise-en-scene

The organization of everything visible inside the frame (set, blocking, light); a layer of language parallel to editing.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

Audiovisual counterpoint

Sound and image deliberately out of sync or set against each other, producing a third meaning.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

Persistence of vision

The retina briefly retains the image, one of the physiological bases of the illusion of motion; frame-by-frame animation exploits it.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

Nonlinear narrative

A story organized out of chronological order; its effect depends on the audience's ability to reconstruct the timeline.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

Genre film

Classes of films organized by convention and audience expectation (westerns, film noir, comedies); a genre is a contract with its audience, and it is constantly rewritten.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

Camera movement

Zoom, dolly, pan, tracking, and following are the basic vocabulary of camera movement; movement changes viewpoint and rhythm and itself carries a command for attention.

Category: Film, Animation and Audiovisual Arts

12 · Digital Media and Game Art

Emergence

Simple rules interacting produce higher-level behavior that is not written into the rules; the core source of interest in games and generative art.

Category: Digital Media and Game Art

The game loop

The core unit of action the player performs over and over; the rhythm and growth of the loop determine the skeleton of the experience.

Category: Digital Media and Game Art

Branching narrative

Player choices lead down different plot paths; true branching is costly, and most implementations are finite-state choices.

Category: Digital Media and Game Art

Emulation and porting

Techniques for running old works in new environments; digital preservation depends on two layers of archive, emulators and the original hardware and software.

Category: Digital Media and Game Art

Algorithmic bias

Training data and the design of objectives carry preference into a system's output; discussing responsibility for AI creation requires documentation of the process.

Category: Digital Media and Game Art

Net art

Art that takes network structure and live data as its medium; works often break as platforms change.

Category: Digital Media and Game Art

User testing

Giving concrete tasks and watching for completion, misreading, and abandonment; usability conclusions come from behavioral evidence, not questionnaires.

Category: Digital Media and Game Art

Game genres

Platformers, role-playing, strategy, shooters, simulations - categories gathered from conventions of rules; a genre is shorthand for a design tradition, not an essential classification.

Category: Digital Media and Game Art

13 · Folk Art and Intermedia

New Year prints (nianhua)

Folk prints pasted up for the festival season; production centers and workshops such as Taohuawu and Yangliuqing formed local styles.

Category: Folk Art and Intermedia

Shehuo festivities and temple fairs

Performance and display within collective festival activity; folk art must be understood together with its occasion.

Category: Folk Art and Intermedia

Oral tradition

Narratives, sung texts, and craft skills handed on by mouth and hand; recording changes the tradition, and preservation and living transmission are two different problems.

Category: Folk Art and Intermedia

The representative-bearer system

The system by which intangible cultural heritage designates representative bearers; it protects transmission while also reshaping its form.

Category: Folk Art and Intermedia

Folk character (minjian)

Spontaneous practice standing outside academy and market (vernacular); "folk" is an analytic category, not a rank of value.

Category: Folk Art and Intermedia

Collective authorship

Many folk works cannot be credited to an individual author; the model of authorship has to be adjusted to workshop, family, and community.

Category: Folk Art and Intermedia

Recontextualization

When folk forms enter the museum or the commodity system, their meaning changes; recording that process is a research responsibility.

Category: Folk Art and Intermedia

Yin and yang cutting

In paper cutting, keeping the lines and cutting away the ground is yang (positive) cutting, cutting away the lines and keeping the ground is yin (negative) cutting; the black-white relation reverses, and the logic parallels relief and intaglio blocks.

Category: Folk Art and Intermedia
