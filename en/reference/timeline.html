Anchor Timeline

127 entries to real artworks and institutions, with China and other traditions side by side; this is not a “greatest works” list but a set of starting points that connect the method lessons to real materials.

How to use the timeline

  1. Pick one or two works per category; look, listen or read first, then record three pieces of evidence with the observation framework of the method lessons.
  2. Note simultaneity: different regions in the same century may be solving entirely different problems — do not rank them as “more advanced.”
  3. Most notes give an institution and a search term (e.g. “search: Maya”): type it into the institution's search box to locate the object; a few entries lack a stable institutional page, and the note states what form the source takes (e.g. “public research materials”).
  4. Collection links point to entry pages of official sites or public-domain archives; the collection page is authoritative for object information — if it disagrees with this page, trust the collection and record the difference.
  5. This page is not a complete art-history narrative; for the systematic study of history, pair it with a general survey of world and Chinese art.
