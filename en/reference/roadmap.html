STUDY / PRACTICE

From Beginner to Independent Practice and Research

The five-stage route now comes with full text, assignments and feedback criteria. “Masterwork study” means studying the methods of mature works and sustained independent practice — not a title conferred by reading.

Nine lessons per category, five stages

StageLessonsObservable learning outcomes
Introductory01–03Can describe artworks with evidence and separate observation from inference.
Foundational04–05Can run controlled comparisons and keep exercise records that can be redone.
Intermediate06–07Can apply the methods of a category to analyze, test, and revise.
Advanced08Can complete a focused project with constraints, process records, and feedback.
Independent project and masterwork study09Can study mature works, complete an independent project, and respond to outside criticism.

Prerequisites run in sequence. Experienced learners may submit outcomes for the relevant stage to self-check and skip ahead after filling the gaps; for wholly unfamiliar fields, starting from lesson 01 is still recommended.

Two study tracks

Humanities track

First complete lessons 01–03 of all 14 categories to build the panorama, then take three categories through lesson 07; at the advanced stage choose artwork analysis, comparative study or a curatorial project rather than becoming a professional maker in each one.

Specialization track

Choose one subfield and concentrate lessons 04–09 on the same theme, completing project after project. After each round, add that field's history, professional technique and original works; build regular feedback with experienced practitioners. This course does not replace the professional studio, music or physical training.

Why a syllabus is not completed master training

Advanced ability requires transfer across subjects, long repeated revision, peer and mentor criticism, and real viewing and use. Finishing the text, answering quiz questions and imitating prompts cannot, alone, prove independent judgment.

Each project round should deliver four things: a reviewable outcome, a traceable process, the strongest objection with your response, and the question for the next round. Only after at least two rounds hold on different objects should you widen the scope.

The yearly studio: repeat without repeating yourself

  1. Round one: limit the medium and the scale, finish one small project, find your weakest ability.
  2. Round two: add basic exercises around that weakness, then a new project on a different object.
  3. Round three: contrast with a different tradition or medium to test the boundaries of the original method.
  4. Round four: assemble a portfolio or research archive, invite criticism from people who know the field, and write the questions for next year.

Rounds need not map onto calendar months — proceed by actual time and feedback; fluency without evidence does not automatically promote to mastery.

Project self-assessment and external feedback

DimensionNeeds redoingCan continue
Question and conceptOnly a broad topic or jargonA specific question is posed, and concepts explain details
Material and evidenceConclusions without locatable groundsAt least three locatable pieces of evidence, sources kept separate from inference
Practice and comparisonOnly the final product, no controlsVersions kept; choices and changes can be explained
Feedback and revisionOnly praise collected, or counterexamples avoidedOne specific objection answered, with the revision explained

All four rows must reach “can continue” before the next stage is advisable; this is a study checklist, not a professional qualification or a master certification. When no reviewer can be found, self-check an anonymized version first and keep external feedback as an open item.
