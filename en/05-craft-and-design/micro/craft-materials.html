MICRO / 05 · Craft Materials

The Temper of Materials and the Working Process

Group micro-lesson · about 15 minutes · Craft Materials
Goal: turn this group of terms into at least three usable observation points and one exercise.

01 · Why this group of terms exists

Every material has its windows of shrinkage, stretch, fusion, and drying; the working process is a negotiation that converts temper into form.

This lesson belongs to the extension layer of Craft and Design; for method foundations see lessons 01–03 of the category.

02 · Observation points

  • Ceramics: The chained variables of clay, glaze, and fire: a deviation in one stage surfaces in the next.
  • Lacquer art: Curing that depends on humidity: raw lacquer polymerizes into film in a moist environment — a slow material shapes a slow craft.
  • Weaving: The mathematics of warp and weft: whether a pattern is feasible is decided by the structure of the loom, not by what one can draw.
  • Metalwork, enamel, and glass: Forging shapes by hammering; casting fills the mold with molten metal; enamel is a glassy fusion onto a metal body; glass depends on blowing and kiln casting. Each material has its own thermal window, tools, and traces; in observation, first assign each trace to its craft.

For term definitions see the core glossary; for entries to real artworks see the anchor timeline.

03 · Do one exercise

Take samples of two materials (paper and cloth are enough), fold the same design in both, and record where each failed and your hypothesis about why.

04 · Acceptance check

Expand after finishing: check your reasoning

Failure is recorded as information about the process, not as "clumsy hands."
