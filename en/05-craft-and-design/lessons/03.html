LESSON 05.03

Visual Hierarchy and Responsibility

Lesson 3 of 9 · 15–25 minutes
Goal of this lesson: use the method of "Visual Hierarchy and Responsibility" to add one more evidence-based observation of a work.

01 · Understand one concept

Type size, spacing, contrast, and arrangement guide the order of reading. Design must also weigh legibility, maintenance, the consumption of resources, and the people left out.

Reading anchor: V&A: collection search. Search sculpture, architecture, or furniture and choose objects with materials and dimensions recorded. The everyday examples and exercises below are an original teaching design, not that institution's own text or authoritative conclusions.

02 · Study one example

If the decoration of an event poster outshines its date and place, it may fail at giving notice however handsome it is.

Return to the lab from lesson one: can the concept of this lesson explain one of the changes you saw there? State the difference between evidence and inference.

03 · Do one exercise

Make two paper versions of an event notice and ask someone to find the time and place within five seconds.

Open after finishing: check your approach

Check three things: whether you completed the assigned comparison; whether you pointed to specific shapes, movements, words, or sounds; whether you kept facts and interpretation apart. Your answer need not match the impressions of the example, as long as you can give grounds.

Check especially in this lesson: A task test checks directly whether the communication succeeded.

04 · Quick self-check

How can you test whether a notice works?

05 · Keep the knowledge

Close this page and explain today's concept in your own words. Tomorrow practice again with a different work, and seven days later set it beside another category. Being able to say the terms is only the start; being able to cite evidence with a different work is transfer.
